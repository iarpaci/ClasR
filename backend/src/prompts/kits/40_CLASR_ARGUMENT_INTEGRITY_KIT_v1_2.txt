## IDENTITY

This kit structures SECTION 4 (Argumentation) output as a
five-axis argument architecture audit. Each axis maps a distinct
question about how the manuscript's argument holds from claim to
conclusion.

Without this kit, S4 produces argument signals as unordered
observations. With this kit, every S4 output follows the A1–A5
schema — systematic, comparable across reports, and traceable
to a specific argument failure type.

---

## CORE PRINCIPLE

An argument has architecture. Architecture can fail at specific
points. This kit names those points.

The five axes are sequential — each depends on the one before it.
A failure at A2 (premises) cascades into A3, A4, and A5.
A failure at A3 (method–claim fit) does not invalidate A1 or A2.
The schema makes cascade failures visible.

---

## THE FIVE AXES

### A1 — CLAIM INTEGRITY
Question: Is the central claim coherent, bounded, and verifiable?

What is checked:
- Is the central claim extractable as a single statement?
- Does the claim carry scope limits (population, context, time)?
- Is the claim falsifiable or testable given the design?
- Is the claim proportionate to the scale of the study?

Primary signals:
  CLAIM_OVERREACH         — claim exceeds what the evidence can support
  SCOPE_UNMARKED          — claim does not carry explicit scope limits
  CONTRIBUTION_ABSENT     — no identifiable contribution claim
  CONTRIBUTION_OVERREACH  — contribution stated beyond what the study establishes

Output format:
```
A1 — CLAIM INTEGRITY
Status: CLEAN / FLAG / CRITICAL
[Signal card if flagged]
Central claim: [extracted one-sentence statement]
Scope declared: YES / PARTIAL / NO
```

### A2 — PREMISE INTEGRITY
Question: Are the load-bearing premises visible, stated, and justified?

What is checked:
- Are the assumptions on which the argument rests made explicit?
- Are implicit causal premises embedded without declaration?
- Are theoretical premises visible or hidden?

Primary signals:
  PREMISE_INVISIBILITY          — load-bearing premise not stated
  IMPLICIT_CAUSAL_PREMISE       — causal assumption embedded without declaration
  THEORETICAL_PREMISE_UNSTATED  — theoretical framework assumed, not declared

Output format:
```
A2 — PREMISE INTEGRITY
Status: CLEAN / FLAG / CRITICAL
[Signal card if flagged]
Visible premises: [list or NONE IDENTIFIED]
Hidden premises: [list or NONE DETECTED]
```

### A3 — METHOD–CLAIM FIT
Question: Does the method test what the claim asserts?

What is checked:
- Does the design type support the claim type?
  (Cross-sectional → association only, not causation)
- Is the operationalization of key constructs adequate?
- Does the analytical approach match the research question?

Primary signals:
  CAUSAL_DESIGN_MISMATCH     — cross-sectional or correlational design
                                with causal or directional claim language
  OPERATIONALIZATION_GAP     — key construct measured by proxy that does
                                not fully capture the construct
  ANALYTICAL_APPROACH_MISMATCH — analysis type does not match
                                   the stated research question
  ASSUMPTION_VALIDITY_SCOPE_MISMATCH — load-bearing assumption validity
                                        range declared or constrained
                                        in methods, but results or
                                        conclusions extend beyond that
                                        range without acknowledgment

ORDINAL_PEARSON_APPLICATION interaction:
  When ORDINAL_PEARSON_APPLICATION is detected in S3, it enters A3
  as an ANALYTICAL_APPROACH_MISMATCH sub-type — the analytical method
  (Pearson correlation assuming interval measurement) does not match
  the variable type (ordinal). The claim that emerges from this
  analysis inherits the assumption mismatch: correlation strengths
  reported for ordinal variables carry an undeclared measurement
  assumption that affects how the finding–claim chain holds.
  Cross-reference note in A3:
  "[→ S3 cross-reference: ORDINAL_PEARSON_APPLICATION detected —
    correlation findings for [variable list] carry undeclared
    interval-measurement assumption. Affects A3 fit assessment.]"
  This note does not create a separate A3 signal — it qualifies
  the existing fit assessment.


ASSUMPTION_VALIDITY_SCOPE_MISMATCH interaction:
  When a manuscript declares or constrains the validity range of a
  load-bearing assumption in the methods section but makes results or
  conclusion claims that extend beyond that declared range without
  explicitly acknowledging the extension, this is an A3 method-claim
  fit failure.

  Trigger conditions (ALL must be met):
    (a) The assumption is load-bearing for the manuscript's central
        measurement, analytical, or interpretive result
    (b) The assumption's validity range is constrained or qualified
        somewhere in the manuscript (methods, footnote, or discussion)
    (c) Results or conclusion claims extend beyond that declared range
        without a statement acknowledging this extension

  Label: ASSUMPTION_VALIDITY_SCOPE_MISMATCH
  Severity: HIGH when central result depends on the assumption;
            MODERATE when a secondary result depends on it
  Section: SECTION 3 (primary) + SECTION 4 A3 (argument consequence)
  PAS eligibility: Tier 2 — auto-elevated by Kit 44 v1.7 Rule C3

  Example: Manuscript states imaging condition is met only at a single
    point on the scintillating screen. Results report emittance values
    derived from the full screen without restricting the claim to the
    validated imaging point. Scope extension is structurally present
    but unacknowledged.

  Non-trigger conditions:
    — Assumption validated across the full range used in results
    — Assumption flagged as limitation with explicit scope note
      in the conclusion section
    — Q-tier calibration places it below detection threshold for
      field-standard implicit assumptions

  Cross-reference:
    Kit 09 §1.13 handles the detection layer (Section 3).
    A3 carries the argument consequence (Section 4).
    Kit 44 v1.7 Rule C3 handles PAS elevation.

  Output format addition to A3 when triggered:
    ASSUMPTION_VALIDITY_SCOPE_MISMATCH — HIGH / MODERATE
    Assumption: [statement of the load-bearing assumption]
    Declared scope: [where and how the scope is constrained]
    Claim extension: [where results/conclusions exceed scope]
    Gap: [one sentence describing the structural mismatch]

SAMPLING_FRAME interaction:
  When SAMPLING_FRAME_ABSENT is detected in S3, it enters S4
  as an A3 signal — it is both a methodological absence (S3) and
  a method–claim fit failure (A3). In the Priority block, it is
  classified as CONTRIBUTION COLLAPSE (Category 1) when the stated
  contribution depends on a population claim the absent sampling
  frame cannot support.

Output format:
```
A3 — METHOD–CLAIM FIT
Status: CLEAN / FLAG / CRITICAL
[Signal card if flagged]
Design type: [cross-sectional / RCT / qualitative / etc.]
Claim type: [associative / causal / exploratory / etc.]
Fit assessment: ALIGNED / TENSION / MISMATCH
[Cross-reference notes if S3 signals interact]
```

### A4 — FINDING–CLAIM ALIGNMENT
Question: Do the findings actually support the stated claim?

What is checked:
- Does the finding language in Results match the claim in Discussion?
- Are results stated with appropriate precision in the conclusion?
- Are complicating findings integrated or selectively deployed?
- Is partial support presented as full confirmation?
- Does a reported finding directionally contradict a policy
  recommendation or intervention target in Discussion/Conclusion?
- Is statistical significance inflated by large sample size while
  practical significance is absent or unreported?

Primary signals:
  ARGUMENT_SLIP              — Results uses associative language;
                                Discussion uses causal language;
                                the gap is not argued

  SELECTIVE_FINDING_DEPLOYMENT — findings that complicate the claim
                                   are not integrated into the argument

  MAGNITUDE_INFLATION        — effect size presented with less
                                precision in conclusions than results

  PARTIAL_SUPPORT_AS_CONFIRMATION — partial evidence treated as
                                       full support for the claim

  FINDING_CONTRADICTS_RECOMMENDATION — a reported finding is
                                directionally opposite to the
                                recommendation or intervention target
                                that follows from it in Discussion
                                or Conclusion.

  Trigger condition (narrow):
    Finding direction in Results AND recommendation direction in
    Discussion/Conclusion must be explicitly extractable AND
    opposite. The signal fires only on directional contradiction —
    not on absence of support, not on weak correlation.

  Example: Results report r=–0.116 between education level and
    genetic test uptake (higher education → less uptake). Discussion
    recommends targeting low-education groups with intervention
    programs. The finding direction (high education = lower uptake)
    contradicts the recommendation premise (low education = the
    gap to close). A reviewer examining Table 2 will identify this.

  Non-example (does not trigger): Finding shows weak positive
    association (r=0.08, p=0.03). Discussion recommends further
    research. No directional contradiction — signal does not fire.

  Severity: HIGH. A directional contradiction between a reported
    finding and a derived recommendation is among the most visible
    argument failures at peer review. Common pattern in KAP
    (Knowledge–Attitude–Practice) studies where subgroup findings
    are selectively deployed to support predetermined targets.

  Priority block placement: Category 1 (Contribution Collapse)
    when the contradiction undermines the manuscript's stated
    practical contribution.

  PRACTICAL_SIGNIFICANCE_ABSENT — statistical significance
                                    reported without effect size,
                                    or effect size present but
                                    trivially small relative to
                                    sample size.

  Trigger conditions (either):
    Condition A: n > 500 AND no effect size reported for
                 primary correlation or association findings.
    Condition B: n > 500 AND effect size present AND
                 |r| < 0.10 (or equivalent Cohen's d < 0.20)
                 while p < 0.05 — statistical significance
                 produced by sample size, not effect magnitude.

  Rationale: In large samples (n > 500), virtually any non-zero
    association reaches statistical significance. A p-value
    without effect size context in this range is systematically
    misleading about the practical magnitude of the finding.

  Format:
  ```
  PRACTICAL_SIGNIFICANCE_ABSENT — MEDIUM / HIGH
  Location: Section 5 (Results) / Section 4 (Discussion)
  n=[sample size]. [Primary finding: r=X, p=Y].
  Effect size [absent / trivially small at r=Z].
  Statistical significance at this n does not establish
  practical significance — [X]% of variance explained.
  ```

  Severity: MEDIUM when effect size simply absent.
             HIGH when effect size present but trivially small
             and the Discussion treats the finding as substantive.

  Interaction with S5: PRACTICAL_SIGNIFICANCE_ABSENT is detected
    in S5 (Numerical Behavior) and cross-referenced in A4 as a
    finding–claim alignment issue. The signal originates in S5;
    A4 carries the argument consequence.

Output format:
```
A4 — FINDING–CLAIM ALIGNMENT
Status: CLEAN / FLAG / CRITICAL
[Signal card for each flagged signal]
Finding language (Results): [associative / causal / hedged]
Claim language (Discussion/Conclusion): [associative / causal / hedged]
Alignment: CONSISTENT / ARGUMENT_SLIP / ESCALATION /
           DIRECTIONAL_CONTRADICTION
Practical significance: [present / absent / trivially small]
```

### A5 — CONCLUSION SCOPE
Question: Does the conclusion stay within what the manuscript
          can actually support?

What is checked:
- Does the conclusion generalize beyond the sample?
- Are limitations acknowledged in the body but ignored
  in the conclusion language?
- Does the conclusion introduce recommendations the
  design cannot support?
- Does the abstract conclusion match the body conclusion?

Primary signals:
  GENERALIZABILITY_CLAIM          — conclusion extends beyond sample
                                    scope without qualification
  LIMITATION_WITHOUT_CONSEQUENCE  — limitations acknowledged in body
                                    but not reflected in conclusion language
  POLICY_OVERREACH                — directive recommendations without
                                    causal or interventional design basis
  ABSTRACT_CONCLUSION_DIVERGENCE  — abstract conclusion does not match
                                    body conclusion

Output format:
```
A5 — CONCLUSION SCOPE
Status: CLEAN / FLAG / CRITICAL
[Signal card if flagged]
Conclusion scope: WITHIN WARRANT / EXPANDED / OVERREACHED
Limitation consistency: REFLECTED / PARTIAL / ABSENT
```

## CASCADE FAILURE LOGIC

When an upstream axis is CRITICAL, downstream axes inherit
a qualification note — not a separate signal, a context tag:

  A1 CRITICAL → A2, A3, A4, A5 all carry: "[→ A1 cascade]"
  A2 CRITICAL → A3, A4, A5 carry: "[→ A2 cascade]"
  A3 CRITICAL → A4, A5 carry: "[→ A3 cascade]"
  A4 CRITICAL → A5 carries: "[→ A4 cascade]"

Cascade tags do not inflate signal count.
They are context notes, not additional signals.

---

## PRIORITY BLOCK INTERACTION

A1 and A3 signals are the most likely to reach the Priority block.

A1 CRITICAL signals → Priority Category 1 (Contribution Collapse)
A3 CRITICAL signals → Priority Category 2 (Structural Absence)
                      or Category 1 if claim depends on sampling frame
A5 signals          → Priority Category 4 (Argument Calibration)
                      when POLICY_OVERREACH or GENERALIZABILITY_CLAIM

A2 and A4 signals rarely reach Priority alone — they are usually
visible as components of A1 or A3 findings.

---

## HARD LIMITS

This kit:
  Does not generate signals from outside S4's reading scope
  Does not cascade severity — only context tags cascade
  Does not suppress clean axes from the summary table
  Does not replace S4 with a score or rating
  Does not produce verdicts
