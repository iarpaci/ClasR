# 35 CLASR — OVERREACH-SIGNAL KIT v1.0
# Kit 35 — Unified Overreach Profile
# CLASR Tier 3 Extension
# Compatible with: CLASR v2.1

---

## 0. IDENTITY

This kit produces a unified overreach profile for every CLASR report.

It classifies detected overreach instances by type and severity,
assembles an aggregate pattern label, and routes each instance
to its primary section.

This kit:
  Does not correct language (governed by Calibration-Deep §1.1–1.4)
  Does not produce verdicts or evaluations
  Does not suppress overreach — it surfaces and classifies it
  Does not recommend revision

---

## 1. DIVISION OF RESPONSIBILITY

### With Calibration-Deep (§1.6 coordination):

Calibration-Deep governs:
  Expression correction — substituting overclaiming language
  with appropriately hedged alternatives.
  This is an active correction behavior, applied silently.

Overreach-Signal Kit governs:
  Severity classification — MINOR / MODERATE / MAJOR
  Type identification — 8 overreach types
  Aggregate pattern — ISOLATED / COMPOUND / SYSTEMATIC
  Unified profile assembly and routing
  This is a signal reporting behavior, not a correction behavior.

Co-occurrence rule:
  When both kits detect the same overreach instance:
  Calibration-Deep applies expression correction silently.
  Overreach-Signal Kit reports severity label visibly.
  Both active simultaneously. Neither suppresses the other.
  Not merged in output.

### With Discussion Scope Drift Kit (CAUSAL_DRIFT collision):
  Scope Drift Kit governs directional drift behavior.
  Overreach-Signal Kit governs severity classification.
  Both active in SECTION 4; not merged.
  Unified profile integrates severity label.

### With Contribution Framing Kit (NOVELTY_OVERREACH / PRIMACY_CLAIM):
  Contribution Framing governs label.
  Overreach-Signal Kit escalates severity if co-occurring overreach present.
  Both active in SECTION 1 as separate sub-signals.

### With LENSSA-DEPTH (GENERALIZATION_OVERREACH / SCALE_SHIFT):
  LENSSA-DEPTH takes precedence on scale-shift detection.
  Overreach-Signal Kit reports severity in unified profile.
  Both active in SECTION 5; not merged.

## 2. OVERREACH TYPES (8)

### Type 1 — CAUSAL_OVERREACH

Definition:
  Causal language applied to a relationship that the design
  does not support (correlational, cross-sectional, observational).

Signal markers:
  "causes", "leads to", "produces", "results in",
  "is responsible for", "drives", "determines"
  — when no experimental or quasi-experimental design is present.

Primary section: SECTION 4
Secondary section: SECTION 6

---

### Type 2 — GENERALIZATION_OVERREACH

Definition:
  Finding generalized beyond the study's population,
  geography, time frame, or sample characteristics
  without acknowledged boundary.

Signal markers:
  "in general", "universally", "for all", "always",
  "globally applicable", "regardless of context"
  — when sample is bounded.

Primary section: SECTION 5
Secondary section: SECTION 4

---

### Type 3 — POLICY_OVERREACH

Definition:
  Findings framed as direct policy mandates or
  action requirements, without conditional framing.

Signal markers:
  "policy must", "authorities should immediately",
  "this finding requires that", "it is necessary for [actor] to"
  — without hedging or conditionality.

Primary section: SECTION 6
Secondary section: SECTION 8

---

### Type 4 — NOVELTY_OVERREACH

Definition:
  Claims of unprecedented originality, first-ever status,
  or unique contribution without comparative basis.

Signal markers:
  "no study has ever", "the first to demonstrate",
  "unprecedented", "never before shown", "unique to this study"
  — without citation support or systematic review basis.

Primary section: SECTION 1
Secondary section: SECTION 4

---

### Type 5 — CONTRIBUTION_OVERREACH

Definition:
  Claimed contribution exceeds what the evidence in the manuscript
  can support. Scope of contribution stated is broader than
  what the findings deliver.

Signal markers:
  "transforms our understanding of", "resolves the debate on",
  "definitively establishes", "fills the major gap in"
  — when findings are partial, contextual, or bounded.

Primary section: SECTION 1
Secondary section: SECTION 0

---

### Type 6 — TEMPORAL_OVERREACH

Definition:
  Cross-sectional or short time-horizon findings framed
  as longitudinal trends or durable patterns without
  acknowledged temporal boundary.

Signal markers:
  "over time", "long-term effect", "sustained impact",
  "persistent pattern" — when design is cross-sectional
  or time window is narrow.

Primary section: SECTION 5
Secondary section: SECTION 4

---

### Type 7 — TRANSLATIONAL_OVERREACH

Definition:
  Findings from one domain, population, or context
  applied directly to a different domain without
  acknowledged translational gap.

Signal markers:
  "these findings apply to clinical practice",
  "directly transferable to", "applicable across all settings"
  — when the original context is bounded or controlled.

Primary section: SECTION 6
Secondary section: SECTION 8

---

### Type 8 — DISCIPLINARY_OVERREACH

Definition:
  Claims that extend into adjacent disciplinary territory
  without methodological grounding in that field's norms
  or without acknowledged disciplinary boundary.

Signal markers:
  "as established in [other field]",
  "consistent with [adjacent discipline]'s conclusion that"
  — when the manuscript does not engage that field's methodology.

Primary section: SECTION 2
Secondary section: SECTION 4

---

## 3. SEVERITY CLASSIFICATION

Each detected overreach instance is assigned one of three severity levels.

### MINOR
  Overreach is present but bounded.
  Language is slightly inflated relative to evidence.
  A reviewer may note it; unlikely to trigger rejection alone.
  Example: mild generalization language without population claim.

### MODERATE
  Overreach is clear and potentially problematic.
  Reviewer attention likely. May require revision.
  Example: causal language in a correlational design,
  clearly policy-directive framing without conditionality.

### MAJOR
  Overreach is significant and structurally embedded.
  Claim is substantially misaligned with evidence base.
  Reviewer attention very likely. Risk of rejection or major revision.
  Example: causal claim central to the argument with no
  experimental basis; novelty claim contradicted by the literature.

### Severity assignment rule:
  Q-Gate modulates severity visibility:
    Q1: MINOR overreach surfaced; MODERATE and MAJOR flagged prominently.
    Q2: MINOR overreach noted conditionally; MODERATE and MAJOR flagged.
    Q3: All severity levels reported; MAJOR flagged with highest priority.
  Field calibration (Journal-Sensitivity Kit) may adjust thresholds
  within Q-Gate range — never below Q-Gate minimum,
  never above MAJOR ceiling.

## 4. AGGREGATE PATTERN

After all overreach instances are classified,
an aggregate pattern label is assigned.

### ISOLATED
  One overreach instance detected.
  Type and severity classified.
  No cross-type co-occurrence.

### COMPOUND
  Two or more overreach instances detected.
  Instances may be of the same or different types.
  Co-occurring but not structurally linked.

### SYSTEMATIC
  Three or more overreach instances detected
  across two or more types, present across multiple sections.
  Pattern suggests structural overreach behavior,
  not isolated expression choice.
  SYSTEMATIC pattern feeds into DESK-REJECT RISK PROFILE
  (LANGUAGE_POSTURE_RISK zone).

### NO OVERREACH DETECTED
  No instances identified across all 8 types.
  Single line produced in SECTION 4.

## 5. ROUTING

Each overreach instance is routed to its primary section
as a sub-signal. The unified profile is assembled in SECTION 0.

```
CAUSAL_OVERREACH        → primary: SECTION 4 / secondary: SECTION 6
GENERALIZATION_OVERREACH → primary: SECTION 5 / secondary: SECTION 4
POLICY_OVERREACH        → primary: SECTION 6 / secondary: SECTION 8
NOVELTY_OVERREACH       → primary: SECTION 1 / secondary: SECTION 4
CONTRIBUTION_OVERREACH  → primary: SECTION 1 / secondary: SECTION 0
TEMPORAL_OVERREACH      → primary: SECTION 5 / secondary: SECTION 4
TRANSLATIONAL_OVERREACH → primary: SECTION 6 / secondary: SECTION 8
DISCIPLINARY_OVERREACH  → primary: SECTION 2 / secondary: SECTION 4
Aggregate pattern       → SECTION 0
```

---

## 7. ACTIVATION

Automatically active for every report.
No user command required.
If no overreach signals detected → single line in SECTION 4, no block in SECTION 0.
If any overreach detected → in-section signals + SECTION 0 unified profile block.

---

## 8. HARD LOCKS

This kit must never:
  Correct or rewrite manuscript language
    (correction governed by Calibration-Deep)
  Produce a verdict on the manuscript's acceptability
  Recommend revision actions
  Suppress any detected overreach instance
  Assign severity above MAJOR
  Assign aggregate pattern without instance evidence

---

## 9. CANONICAL DEFINITION

OVERREACH-SIGNAL-KIT v1.0 classifies overreach instances
across 8 types (CAUSAL, GENERALIZATION, POLICY, NOVELTY,
CONTRIBUTION, TEMPORAL, TRANSLATIONAL, DISCIPLINARY),
assigns severity (MINOR / MODERATE / MAJOR) per instance,
produces an aggregate pattern label (ISOLATED / COMPOUND / SYSTEMATIC),
routes in-section signals to primary sections,
and assembles a unified OVERREACH PROFILE block in SECTION 0 —
without correcting language, producing verdicts,
or modifying any other system behavior.
