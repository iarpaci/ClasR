## 0. IDENTITY

Type: EXTENSION KIT
Attaches to: CLASR reading layer (CORE unchanged)
No content generation · No judgment · No recommendation

This kit detects verbal ambiguity, procedural opacity,
and meritless validation language in methodology sections
of qualitative, interpretive, mixed-method, and critical
academic manuscripts.

It does not evaluate whether the methodology is correct.
It surfaces how methodological claims are verbally constructed —
specifically where language substitutes for methodological
transparency.

**Scope by manuscript type:**

  PRIMARY ACTIVATION — full signal detection:
    Qualitative empirical (ethnography, discourse analysis,
    grounded theory, phenomenology, thematic analysis)
    Mixed-method (qualitative-dominant or equal-weight)
    Critical / normative (critical theory, policy analysis,
    feminist methodology, action research)
    Interpretive theoretical (where methodology section exists)

  CONDITIONAL ACTIVATION — framing zone only:
    VERBAL-PRIMARY without explicit methodology section
    → procedural opacity and legitimation by label
      readable from method references in framing zones
    QUANTITATIVE-PRIMARY
    → this kit reads verbal framing of quantitative methods
      only when explicit verbal risk signals are present
      (e.g., "rigorous analysis was conducted" without specification)

  SILENT:
    Pure theoretical manuscripts with no methodology section
    → kit runs silently; no note produced

---

## 1. DESIGN PRINCIPLE

CLASR's SECTION 3 (Methodological Visibility) reads
whether a method exists and how transparent it is.
ARGUMENT-INTEGRITY-KIT (40) reads method-claim fit
at the structural level.

Neither reads verbal behavior within the methodology section
itself — where language may perform methodological rigor
without delivering it.

In qualitative and mixed-method manuscripts, methodology
sections carry a specific verbal risk profile:
terms invoke established methods without operationalizing them,
quality claims are made without grounding, procedural steps
are described so generally that replication is impossible.

This kit reads that verbal layer.

```
Methodology section identified
         ↓
Verbal behavior patterns scanned
         ↓
Five signal types detected independently
         ↓
Q-variant calibration applied
         ↓
Signals routed to SECTION 3
```

---

## 2. SIGNAL TAXONOMY

Five signal types. Each is independent — one may fire
without others.

---

### 2.1 PROCEDURAL_OPACITY

Methodological procedures described so generally that
an independent reader could not replicate or assess them.

**Signal indicators:**
- "Analysis was conducted" without specifying what analysis
- "Themes emerged from the data" without describing the process
- "The data was coded" without explaining the coding procedure
- "An iterative process was used" without stages defined
- "Following established procedures" without naming them
- Passive constructions that remove the researcher from
  methodological decisions

**Threshold:** Any instance where the core analytical procedure
is named but not described at a level permitting independent
assessment.

**Intensity:**
  LOW: minor procedural gap; core procedure described elsewhere
  MEDIUM: key procedure underdescribed; partial specification
  HIGH: core analytical procedure functionally opaque;
        independent assessment not possible from text

---

### 2.2 SATURATION_CLAIM_WITHOUT_GROUNDING

Saturation, sufficiency, or data adequacy is claimed
without stating the criterion by which it was determined.

**Signal indicators:**
- "Data saturation was reached" without criterion
- "Theoretical saturation was achieved" without process
- "No new themes emerged" without describing the point
  at which this was determined or how many cases were needed
- "The sample was sufficient" without adequacy rationale
- "Saturation was confirmed by [X]" where X is circular
  (e.g., the researcher's judgment, without further grounding)

**Note on field norms:**
In some fields (e.g., constructivist grounded theory),
saturation is a recognized concept with established literature.
This kit reads whether the claim is grounded in that literature
or stated as self-evident. "Saturation was reached" alone
is ungrounded; "saturation was reached per [specific criterion]
as defined in [source]" is grounded.

**Intensity:**
  LOW: saturation claimed; partial criterion visible
  MEDIUM: saturation claimed; criterion absent but field norm present
  HIGH: saturation claimed; no criterion; field norm absent

---

### 2.3 LEGITIMATION_BY_LABEL

A recognized method or framework is named as if
the naming itself constitutes methodological justification —
without describing how the method was operationalized
in this specific study.

**Signal indicators:**
- "Grounded theory was used" without describing procedures
- "Discourse analysis was applied" without analytical framework
- "A phenomenological approach was adopted" without
  describing the phenomenological reduction or epoché
- "Thematic analysis following [author]" without
  describing how [author]'s framework was applied
- "An interpretivist paradigm informed the study"
  without methodological consequences being stated

**Distinction from adequate citation:**
Legitimation by label = name only, no operationalization.
Adequate use = name + how it was operationalized here.

**Intensity:**
  LOW: method named with partial operationalization
  MEDIUM: method named; operationalization absent
  HIGH: multiple methods named; none operationalized

---

### 2.4 RIGOR_VOCABULARY_WITHOUT_PRACTICE

Quality assurance language from qualitative research
traditions is invoked without corresponding practice
being described.

**Signal indicators:**
- "Trustworthiness was ensured" without stating how
- "Credibility was established through [X]" where X
  is a generic term without specific practice
  (e.g., "member checking" mentioned but not described)
- "Validity was addressed" in a qualitative manuscript
  without specifying which validity concept and how
- "Reflexivity was maintained throughout" without
  evidence of how reflexivity was operationalized
- "Rigorous analysis" used as a quality claim without
  describing what rigor consisted of in this context

**Note on terminology:**
"Validity," "reliability," and "rigor" carry different meanings
across paradigms. This kit reads whether the invoked term
is used with paradigm-appropriate grounding — not whether
the term itself is used.

**Intensity:**
  LOW: quality term used; partial practice described
  MEDIUM: quality term used; practice absent
  HIGH: multiple quality terms invoked; none grounded

---

### 2.5 SAMPLING_CIRCULARITY

The sample is justified by criteria that are circular —
the sample is described as appropriate because it produced
the findings, or the selection criteria reproduce the
conclusions they were meant to test.

**Signal indicators:**
- "Participants were selected because they had experience
  with X" where X is the phenomenon the study claims to discover
- "Purposive sampling was used to identify individuals
  who exhibited [the pattern under study]"
  without pre-sampling criteria being stated
- "The sample was appropriate given the findings"
  (retrospective justification)
- "Snowball sampling was used" without describing
  starting criteria or stopping rules

**Intensity:**
  LOW: sampling rationale partially circular
  MEDIUM: sampling criteria imply the conclusion
  HIGH: explicit retrospective justification present

## 4. SECTION ROUTING

| Signal | Primary SECTION | Secondary SECTION |
|--------|----------------|------------------|
| PROCEDURAL_OPACITY | SECTION 3 | SECTION 8 |
| SATURATION_CLAIM_WITHOUT_GROUNDING | SECTION 3 | SECTION 8 |
| LEGITIMATION_BY_LABEL | SECTION 3 | — |
| RIGOR_VOCABULARY_WITHOUT_PRACTICE | SECTION 3 | SECTION 6 |
| SAMPLING_CIRCULARITY | SECTION 3 | SECTION 4 |

SECTION 8 routing:
PROCEDURAL_OPACITY and SATURATION_CLAIM_WITHOUT_GROUNDING
create unmapped knowledge boundaries — independent readers
cannot assess the methodological basis of findings.
These route to SECTION 8 as limit signals.

SECTION 6 routing:
RIGOR_VOCABULARY_WITHOUT_PRACTICE is also a language-level
signal — quality claims without grounding are a form
of epistemic overreach. Routes to SECTION 6 as secondary.

SECTION 4 routing:
SAMPLING_CIRCULARITY creates an argumentative integrity gap —
findings cannot be cleanly separated from sampling criteria.
Routes to SECTION 4 as secondary.

This kit does not open new SECTIONS.

## 5. Q-VARIANT CALIBRATION

**Q1:**
All five signals active at HIGH sensitivity.
PROCEDURAL_OPACITY at any instance flagged.
SATURATION_CLAIM_WITHOUT_GROUNDING flagged at HIGH sensitivity
— Q1 reviewers require explicit saturation criteria.
LEGITIMATION_BY_LABEL flagged at HIGH sensitivity
— Q1 requires operationalization, not naming.
RIGOR_VOCABULARY_WITHOUT_PRACTICE at HIGH sensitivity.
SAMPLING_CIRCULARITY flagged at any detection.

**Q2:**
All signals active at standard sensitivity.
PROCEDURAL_OPACITY at MEDIUM and HIGH flagged.
SATURATION_CLAIM_WITHOUT_GROUNDING at MEDIUM and HIGH flagged.
LEGITIMATION_BY_LABEL at MEDIUM and HIGH flagged.
LOW intensity signals noted without escalation.

**Q3:**
PROCEDURAL_OPACITY at HIGH flagged.
SATURATION_CLAIM_WITHOUT_GROUNDING: if field standard
  for saturation claims exists and is followed → suppressed.
  If no field standard engaged → MEDIUM flagged.
LEGITIMATION_BY_LABEL at HIGH flagged.
LOW and MEDIUM intensity signals suppressed silently.

## 6. PARTIAL INPUT BEHAVIOR

**Abstract only (Type A):**
→ Kit largely not assessable — methodology not visible
→ If abstract contains method reference: LEGITIMATION_BY_LABEL
  readable at LOW confidence
→ Label: [Methodological verbal risk — abstract only,
   full assessment requires methods section]

**Introduction only (Type B):**
→ LEGITIMATION_BY_LABEL partially readable
→ Other signals not assessable

**Methods only (Type C):**
→ Full activation — primary input type for this kit
→ All five signals fully readable

**Results / Discussion only (Type D):**
→ SAMPLING_CIRCULARITY partially readable from discussion
→ Other signals not assessable

**Mixed (Type E):**
→ Kit runs on available methodology zone
→ Coverage declared

## 7. COLLISION MANAGEMENT

### With Argument Integrity Kit (40) — A3:

Argument Integrity Kit A3 reads method-claim fit:
whether the statistical method matches the study design.

This kit reads verbal behavior within the methodology section:
whether methodological language is grounded.

Both active simultaneously; different analytical levels.
Where both fire (e.g., CAUSAL_DESIGN_MISMATCH + PROCEDURAL_OPACITY):
→ Both reported in SECTION 3 as separate sub-signals.
→ Not merged.

---

### With Epistemic Frame Kit (47):

Epistemic Frame Kit reads EPISTEMIC_PRIVILEGE —
methodological choices presented as neutral or obvious.

This kit reads LEGITIMATION_BY_LABEL —
methodological choices named but not operationalized.

Both may fire on the same methodology zone:
→ Epistemic Frame Kit: EPISTEMIC_PRIVILEGE in SECTION 3
→ This kit: LEGITIMATION_BY_LABEL in SECTION 3
→ Both reported; not merged.

---

### With Calibration-Deep Kit (09):

Calibration-Deep reads statistical method-design mismatches
(§1.9: ORDINAL_PEARSON_APPLICATION, LARGE_SAMPLE_NORMALITY_TEST).

This kit reads verbal construction of methodology —
not statistical method choice.

Both active; non-overlapping domains.
Not merged.

---

### With Hedging Calibration Kit (37):

Hedging Calibration reads RIGOR_VOCABULARY hedging consistency.
This kit reads RIGOR_VOCABULARY_WITHOUT_PRACTICE as a
methodology-section-specific signal.

Where both fire on the same quality claim:
→ Hedging Calibration: CALIBRATION_FAILURE in SECTION 6
→ This kit: RIGOR_VOCABULARY_WITHOUT_PRACTICE in SECTION 3
→ Both active; reported in respective sections; not merged.

---

## 8. REVISION ROUND INTERACTION

In Revision Round Mode, methodological verbal risk signals
carry additional weight under Q1-level sensitivity.

PROCEDURAL_OPACITY and LEGITIMATION_BY_LABEL in revised
manuscripts are read as residual risk — methodological
transparency is among the most common R1 reviewer demands
for qualitative and interpretive submissions.

SATURATION_CLAIM_WITHOUT_GROUNDING in revised manuscripts
is particularly weighted: reviewers who raised it in R1
will verify whether a criterion was added.

No separate flag produced.
Signal reported as normal; sensitivity applies implicitly.

---

## 9. HARD LIMITS

This kit must never:
  Evaluate whether the methodology is appropriate
    for the research question
  Judge which qualitative method should have been used
  Recommend specific methodological procedures
  Label the methodology as weak or flawed
  Compare the methodology to field standards
    beyond what is visible in the manuscript text
  Attribute methodological choices to author ignorance
    or deception
  Generate independent sections outside the routing table

---

## 10. WHAT THIS KIT DOES NOT DO

Does not:
  Evaluate methodological rigor or sophistication
  Recommend specific grounding strategies
  Assess whether saturation was actually reached
  Compare sampling to field norms not visible in the text
  Produce a methodology quality score

---

## 11. CANONICAL DEFINITION

METHODOLOGICAL VERBAL RISK KIT v1.0 detects five verbal
risk patterns in qualitative, interpretive, mixed-method,
and critical manuscript methodology sections —
procedural opacity, saturation claims without grounding,
legitimation by label, rigor vocabulary without practice,
and sampling circularity — producing a structured signal
profile in SECTION 3 with secondary routing to SECTION 4,
SECTION 6, and SECTION 8 —
without evaluating methodological correctness, recommending
procedures, or producing quality judgments.
