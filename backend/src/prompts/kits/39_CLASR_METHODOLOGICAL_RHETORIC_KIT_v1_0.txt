## 1. PURPOSE (ONE SENTENCE)

To surface field-independent methodological rhetoric patterns —
argumentative moves that use methodological framing
to perform validation, suppress uncertainty, or reframe
inter-method disagreement — as structural behavior signals,
without producing evaluation of the methods themselves.

---

## 2. DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Calibration-Deep reads over-claim and normative language.
Overreach Signal Kit classifies overreach by type and severity.
Concept-Evidence Bridge reads whether concepts are grounded.
Argument Symmetry reads claim-to-limit proportionality.

None of these reads the specific rhetorical moves
by which methodological choices are presented
as self-validating, visually confirmed, or mutually complementary
in ways that suppress legitimate uncertainty.

These patterns are not field-specific.
They appear across quantitative disciplines —
physics, chemistry, ecology, economics, medicine —
wherever methods carry argumentative weight
beyond what they structurally deliver.

```
Methodological framing zone scanned
         ↓
Rhetoric pattern type identified
         ↓
Structural behavior mapped:
  what the move performs vs. what it claims to perform
         ↓
Signal reported — no verdict on the method itself
```

## 3. SIGNAL CATALOGUE (4 PATTERNS)

---

### 3.1 REPRESENTATIVENESS_TRANSFER

**Definition:**
An assumption is made that a measured or sampled subset
fully represents a broader population, domain, or condition —
and this representativeness is treated as established
rather than argued.

**Structural behavior:**
The manuscript proceeds as if the sample-to-population
transfer has been validated, when it has only been assumed.
The assumption carries argumentative weight
it has not earned through evidence or acknowledged uncertainty.

**Signal indicators:**
- A single energy slice, time window, spatial zone, or
  experimental condition treated as representative of
  the full distribution without boundary acknowledgment
- "Each [subset] represents [whole]" stated without
  evidence of representativeness testing
- Results from a bounded sample generalized to
  a broader context where the transfer is load-bearing
  for the manuscript's main claim
- Absence of sensitivity analysis for representativeness assumption

**Distinction from GENERALIZATION_OVERREACH (Kit 35):**
GENERALIZATION_OVERREACH flags when results are
generalized beyond scope in the claim layer.
REPRESENTATIVENESS_TRANSFER flags when the representativeness
assumption is embedded in the method layer —
before claims are made — and operates as a silent premise.

**Primary section:** SECTION 3
**Secondary section:** SECTION 4

---

### 3.2 VISUAL_CHECK_AS_VALIDATION

**Definition:**
A figure, plot, or visual output is used as evidence
of methodological validity — with the visual inspection
performing the role of a formal validation procedure
that was not conducted.

**Structural behavior:**
The manuscript presents a figure and either explicitly
or implicitly treats visual regularity, symmetry, or
expected pattern as confirmation that a methodological
assumption holds or a procedure is valid.
No quantitative validation is present or referenced.

**Signal indicators:**
- "Figure N shows / confirms / demonstrates that [method assumption]"
  where Figure N is a distributional plot, scatter, or output map
- "Visual inspection suggests the assumption is satisfied"
  as the only validation statement for a critical assumption
- A figure described as "consistent with" a methodological requirement
  without any quantitative test of that consistency
- "As can be seen from [Figure N], the method performs as expected"
  without performance metrics

**Distinction from pseudo-evidence (CLASR CORE):**
The core "map is not evidence" principle applies to
spatial and numerical visuals used as argumentative conclusions.
VISUAL_CHECK_AS_VALIDATION applies specifically to
visuals used as methodological validation instruments —
where the claim is not about findings but about method adequacy.

**Primary section:** SECTION 3
**Secondary section:** SECTION 5

---

### 3.3 DISTRIBUTIONAL_CONFIRMATION_BIAS

**Definition:**
A distributional output — histogram, density plot,
residual distribution, cluster visualization — is
interpreted as supporting a methodological assumption
using hedged language that nonetheless performs
a confirmatory function the evidence cannot sustain.

**Structural behavior:**
The manuscript presents a distribution and uses
qualified language ("appears to support", "is consistent with",
"suggests that") to draw a methodological conclusion
that goes beyond what distributional inspection can establish.
The hedge is present but functions as rhetorical cover
rather than genuine uncertainty acknowledgment.

**Distinction from HEDGE_STRIPPING (Kit 37):**
Hedging Calibration Kit reads when hedges are removed
as claims move through the manuscript.
DISTRIBUTIONAL_CONFIRMATION_BIAS reads when a hedge
is present but structurally performs a confirmatory
function — the hedge does not constrain the claim
it nominally qualifies.

**Signal indicators:**
- "The distribution appears to support [assumption X]"
  where X is a load-bearing methodological premise
  and no formal test of X is conducted
- "Figure N is consistent with the [normality / independence /
  stationarity] assumption" as the sole validation statement
- Distribution described as "roughly [expected pattern]"
  followed by a methodological conclusion treated as established
- Hedged visual interpretation in methods section;
  unhedged methodological conclusion in results or discussion

**Calibration-Deep coordination (§1.3):**
Calibration-Deep §1.3 reads uncertainty suppression
at the level of SECTION 8 absence.
DISTRIBUTIONAL_CONFIRMATION_BIAS reads uncertainty suppression
embedded in the method validation layer —
where the uncertainty is nominally acknowledged
but structurally neutralized.
Both active simultaneously; not merged.

**Primary section:** SECTION 3
**Secondary section:** SECTION 4

---

### 3.4 COMPLEMENTARITY_REFRAMING

**Definition:**
When two methods, models, or analytical approaches
produce divergent or inconsistent results,
the divergence is reframed as "complementarity" —
the methods are described as addressing different aspects
of the same phenomenon — without acknowledging that
the inconsistency constitutes an unresolved methodological tension.

**Structural behavior:**
This is the most structurally significant pattern in this kit.
The manuscript has two methods that disagree.
Rather than treating the disagreement as a signal
requiring explanation or boundary acknowledgment,
the manuscript performs a reframing: the methods are
not contradicting each other, they are complementing each other.
This move suppresses the epistemological question
the disagreement raises.

**Signal indicators:**
- "Method A and Method B yield different results,
  but they address different aspects of the problem"
  — without specifying which aspects and why
  the difference in results follows from that
- "The two approaches are complementary rather than contradictory"
  — stated as conclusion, not demonstrated
- Inter-method disagreement acknowledged in methods or results,
  then resolved through framing in discussion
  without quantitative or theoretical justification
- "This discrepancy reflects the different sensitivities
  of the two methods" — sensitivity difference asserted,
  not measured or cited

**Distinction from CAUSAL_OVERREACH (Kit 35):**
CAUSAL_OVERREACH flags when causal language exceeds
the design's causal capacity.
COMPLEMENTARITY_REFRAMING flags when framing language
resolves a methodological inconsistency
that the evidence does not resolve.

**Distinction from Discussion Scope Drift MECHANISM_INTRODUCTION:**
Discussion Scope Drift reads when new mechanisms are introduced
in the discussion without results support.
COMPLEMENTARITY_REFRAMING reads when an existing disagreement
is dissolved through framing — the rhetorical move
is not addition but suppression.

**Primary section:** SECTION 3
**Secondary section:** SECTION 4

## 5. Q-VARIANT CALIBRATION

Q1:
  All four patterns active at full sensitivity.
  COMPLEMENTARITY_REFRAMING and REPRESENTATIVENESS_TRANSFER
  explicitly surfaced — Q1 reviewers in methods-intensive fields
  scrutinize these moves closely.
  SYSTEMATIC density noted as potential reviewer concern.

Q2:
  All patterns active at standard sensitivity.
  COMPLEMENTARITY_REFRAMING noted without escalation.
  DISTRIBUTIONAL_CONFIRMATION_BIAS noted at standard detail.

Q3:
  COMPLEMENTARITY_REFRAMING and REPRESENTATIVENESS_TRANSFER reported.
  VISUAL_CHECK_AS_VALIDATION and DISTRIBUTIONAL_CONFIRMATION_BIAS
  reported only if structurally load-bearing.

## 6. FIELD SENSITIVITY

These four patterns are field-independent by design.
They appear in physics, chemistry, ecology, economics,
medicine, engineering, and social science quantitative work.

Journal-Sensitivity Kit (Kit 38) may modulate
detection threshold for specific journals where
visual validation or complementarity framing
is a recognized disciplinary convention.

When Journal-Sensitivity Kit is active:
→ Field-conventional use of visual validation noted
   at reduced signal intensity (not suppressed)
→ COMPLEMENTARITY_REFRAMING remains at full sensitivity
   regardless of field convention

## 8. REVISION ROUND INTERACTION

In Revision Round Mode, methodological rhetoric signals
carry additional weight under Q1-level sensitivity.

COMPLEMENTARITY_REFRAMING in revised manuscripts is read
as residual risk — inter-method disagreement framing
is a frequent target of substantive R1 reviewer comments
in methods-intensive fields.

REPRESENTATIVENESS_TRANSFER in revised manuscripts
is read as residual risk when it is load-bearing
for the manuscript's primary claim.

No separate flag produced.
Signal reported as normal; sensitivity applies implicitly.

## 10. COLLISION MANAGEMENT

If REPRESENTATIVENESS_TRANSFER overlaps with
Overreach Signal Kit GENERALIZATION_OVERREACH (Kit 35):
→ Overreach Kit governs claim-layer severity;
  this kit reports method-layer assumption behavior
→ Both active in SECTION 3 and SECTION 4; not merged

If VISUAL_CHECK_AS_VALIDATION overlaps with
Concept-Evidence Bridge Kit OPERATIONALIZATION_GAP (Kit 33):
→ Concept-Evidence Bridge governs concept-level grounding;
  this kit reports visual-validation rhetoric behavior
→ Both active; reported as separate sub-signals

If DISTRIBUTIONAL_CONFIRMATION_BIAS overlaps with
Calibration-Deep §1.3 uncertainty suppression:
→ Calibration-Deep governs SECTION 8 production threshold;
  this kit reports method-validation layer suppression
→ Both active; not merged

If DISTRIBUTIONAL_CONFIRMATION_BIAS overlaps with
Hedging Calibration Kit HEDGE_STRIPPING (Kit 37):
→ Hedging Calibration Kit governs hedge consistency pattern;
  this kit reports distributional confirmation behavior
→ Both may fire; reported as separate sub-signals in SECTION 3

If COMPLEMENTARITY_REFRAMING overlaps with
Discussion Scope Drift Kit MECHANISM_INTRODUCTION (Kit 24):
→ Scope Drift Kit governs new mechanism introduction;
  this kit governs disagreement-suppression reframing
→ Both active in SECTION 4; reported as separate sub-signals

If COMPLEMENTARITY_REFRAMING overlaps with
Argument Symmetry Kit ASYMMETRIC CORE zone (Kit 25):
→ Symmetry Kit governs zone-level ratio;
  this kit governs the specific reframing move
→ Both active; not merged

---

## 11. WHAT THIS KIT DOES NOT DO

Does not:
  Evaluate whether the methods are appropriate or valid
  Judge whether visual inspection is scientifically acceptable
  Assess whether complementarity framing is justified
  Recommend specific validation procedures
  Label the manuscript as methodologically flawed
  Generate independent flags outside SECTION 3 and SECTION 4

---

## 12. HARD LIMITS

This kit must never:
  Evaluate methodological correctness
  Recommend specific statistical tests or validation approaches
  Label methods as inappropriate or insufficient
  Produce a verdict on the manuscript's methodological quality
  Act as a standalone methods review tool

---

## 13. CANONICAL DEFINITION

METHODOLOGICAL RHETORIC KIT v1.0 surfaces four
field-independent methodological rhetoric patterns —
REPRESENTATIVENESS_TRANSFER, VISUAL_CHECK_AS_VALIDATION,
DISTRIBUTIONAL_CONFIRMATION_BIAS, and COMPLEMENTARITY_REFRAMING —
as structural behavior signals that map how methodological
framing performs validation, suppresses uncertainty,
or reframes inter-method disagreement beyond what
the evidence structurally supports — without producing
judgment, evaluation, or methodological recommendation.
