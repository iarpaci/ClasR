## IDENTITY

The Executive Summary Block produces a 5–7 sentence plain-language
summary of the manuscript's most critical signals. It appears before
PRIORITY ACTION SIGNALS and before SECTION 0.

Purpose: allow the reader to grasp the manuscript's risk profile
and primary signal locations without reading the full report.

This block is not a verdict. It is not an editorial recommendation.
It is a navigational aid — a map of where the report's weight sits.

## CONTENT RULES

The block contains exactly 5–7 sentences. No more, no less.

Sentence 1: Manuscript type, field, and Q-profile in one sentence.
Sentence 2: The single most critical signal — what it is and where.
Sentence 3: The second most critical signal — what it is and where.
Sentence 4: A third signal OR a pattern observation connecting
            two signals if they interact. EXCEPTION: if a HIGH
            convergence signal (×4+) exists (53_CLASR_LAYER_CONVERGENCE_KIT
            active), this sentence references the convergence —
            "The most critical signal carries HIGH convergence —
            four independent analysis layers flag [brief element
            description]." — replacing the generic pattern observation.
Sentence 5: Overall structural or argumentative risk profile —
            one sentence characterizing the manuscript's dominant
            weakness or tension.
Sentence 6 (optional): Reproducibility or integrity risk if
            M-tier signals are critical.
Sentence 7 (optional): A counterbalancing strength signal if
            present — what the manuscript does well. Never omitted
            if a genuine strength exists.

---

## LANGUAGE RULES

- Editorial register — not technical signal vocabulary
- No signal labels in this block (SIGNAL_LABEL format belongs
  to PRIORITY ACTION SIGNALS only)
- No hedging inflation — one qualifier per uncertainty
- No verdict language — no "should", "must", "recommend"
- Active voice preferred
- Section references permitted: "in the methods section",
  "in the conclusion" — not "SECTION 3" or "SECTION 6"

---

## HARD LIMITS

This block:
  Does not replace PRIORITY ACTION SIGNALS
  Does not replace any SECTION
  Does not produce recommendations
  Does not assign scores
  Cannot exceed 7 sentences
  Cannot be omitted once this kit is active

## EXAMPLES

### Example A — QUANTITATIVE-PRIMARY (Palestinian KAP study)

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
This is a cross-sectional KAP survey in public health genomics,
targeting Q1 (Scientific Reports, Nature Portfolio), with a
quantitative-primary design. The most significant signal is a
missing sampling frame — the study claims national-level coverage
of Palestine but documents no enumeration of the source population
or verified governorate-level representation. The second critical
signal is the absence of multivariate analysis: multiple demographic
predictors are tested against multiple outcomes using bivariate
tests only, and the discussion language then implies independent
predictor identification the design cannot support. These two
signals combine to produce a structural gap between what the
methods establish and what the discussion and conclusion claim.
Confidence intervals and effect sizes are absent across all four
tables, a gap that will register immediately at Q1 under APA 7
and STROBE standards. The study's strengths are its sample size,
transparent null result reporting, and clear IRB documentation.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Example B — VERBAL-PRIMARY (UK court curbing article)

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
This is a theory-guided single-country case study in comparative
constitutional law, published in a Q1 venue (American Journal of
Comparative Law), with a verbal-primary design. The central
argumentative tension is that the paper's empirical anchor —
that publics prefer narrowly targeted court curbing over broadly
targeted measures — rests on a single US-based study, and the
mechanism's application to the UK is asserted rather than
independently supported. The second critical signal sits in Part II:
the Conservative Party's "enduring reverence" for the judiciary is
established through selective primary source quotation (Blackstone,
Disraeli, Churchill, Thatcher) with minimal secondary historical
scholarship to validate the pattern as a genuine ideational
constraint rather than rhetorical performance. These two signals
interact — both the structural mechanism and the ideational
constraint are argued from thin independent evidential bases,
making the paper's explanatory framework more illustrative than
confirmatory, which the author acknowledges but does not fully
resolve. The paper's scope discipline is a genuine strength:
the single-country framing is honest, the generalizing ambitions
are clearly flagged as exploratory, and the seven-case analysis
is systematically structured.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
