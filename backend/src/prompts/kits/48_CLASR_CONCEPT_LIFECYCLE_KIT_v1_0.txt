## 0. IDENTITY

Type: EXTENSION KIT
Attaches to: CLASR reading layer (CORE unchanged)
No content generation · No judgment · No recommendation

This kit tracks the full lifecycle of key concepts
across a manuscript — from initial definition through
expansion, metaphorization, and eventual epistemic role —
as a structural concept behavior map.

It does not evaluate whether concept evolution is appropriate.
It does not judge theoretical choices.
It maps how concepts move, transform, and accumulate
argumentative weight across the manuscript's full length.

**Scope by manuscript type:**
  VERBAL-PRIMARY   → full lifecycle tracking; all 5 concepts
  HYBRID           → full lifecycle tracking; all 5 concepts
  QUANTITATIVE-PRIMARY → framing zone only (introduction +
                         discussion); STAGE 0, 1, and conclusion
                         role assessable; STAGE 2–5 limited

---

## 1. DESIGN PRINCIPLE

CLASR's Concept-Evidence Bridge Kit (33) reads
whether key concepts are grounded in evidence —
mapping GROUNDED / PARTIAL / FLOATING status at a point in time.

This kit reads differently:
It tracks the same concept longitudinally —
how it enters, how it is defined (or not),
how it is applied, how it transforms,
and what argumentative role it performs by the conclusion.

A concept in a theoretical manuscript does not simply
"expand" — it passes through identifiable stages.
Tracking this lifecycle makes visible:
- where a concept gains argumentative load it was not given
- where a concept's meaning silently shifts between sections
- where a concept becomes a vehicle for claims
  the manuscript cannot otherwise support

```
Key concepts identified from framing zone
         ↓
Each concept tracked across all manuscript zones
         ↓
Lifecycle stage mapped at each appearance
         ↓
Transformation events identified
         ↓
Final epistemic role assessed
         ↓
Lifecycle profile reported per concept — no verdict
```

---

## 2. KEY CONCEPT IDENTIFICATION

Key concepts are identified from:
- Title vocabulary
- Abstract claim-carrying terms
- Introduction's definitional zone
- Theoretical framework's central terms
- Terms with HIGH frequency density (FREQUENCY SIGNAL KIT)
- Terms that appear in both framing zone and conclusion

Maximum tracked concepts per manuscript: 5
If more than 5 qualify, the 5 most claim-carrying
concepts are selected based on frequency and
argumentative position.

Proper nouns, author names, and field names
are excluded from tracking.

---

## 3. LIFECYCLE STAGE TAXONOMY

Each concept appearance is mapped to a lifecycle stage:

```
STAGE 0 — INTRODUCTION
  Concept appears for the first time.
  Context: framing zone, abstract, or introduction.

STAGE 1 — DEFINITION
  Concept is given an explicit definition or boundary.
  Sub-states:
    DEFINED_EXPLICIT  → clear definitional statement present
    DEFINED_IMPLICIT  → definition implied by context
    UNDEFINED         → concept used without definition

STAGE 2 — APPLICATION
  Concept is applied to the manuscript's analytical object.
  Sub-states:
    OPERATIONALIZED → concept connected to observable indicator
    ILLUSTRATIVE    → concept used to frame, not analyze
    ASSUMED         → concept applied as if already established

STAGE 3 — EXTENSION
  Concept is applied beyond its initial scope.
  Sub-states:
    MARKED_EXTENSION   → scope expansion is acknowledged
    UNMARKED_EXTENSION → scope expansion is silent

STAGE 4 — METAPHORIZATION
  Concept is used in a metaphorical or analogical register —
  its original meaning is carried by rhetorical transfer
  rather than analytical specification.
  Signal: concept applied to domains structurally
  different from its origin without bridge language.

STAGE 5 — EPISTEMIC CARRIER
  Concept has accumulated sufficient rhetorical weight
  that it performs argumentative functions independently
  of its definition or evidence base.
  Signal: concept used to close an argument
  ("this confirms the X dynamic") without
  the argument being made explicitly.

STAGE 6 — CONCLUSION ROLE
  How the concept functions in the manuscript's conclusion.
  Sub-states:
    ANCHORING   → conclusion centers on this concept
    SUPPORTING  → concept supports a broader conclusion
    ABSENT      → concept introduced but absent from conclusion
    SUBSTITUTED → concept replaced by a different term
                  that carries the same function
```

---

## 4. TRANSFORMATION EVENTS

Between lifecycle stages, five transformation events are tracked:

```
SEMANTIC_DRIFT
  The concept's meaning shifts between appearances
  without explicit acknowledgment.
  Example: "resilience" shifts from ecological
  to psychological to social meaning across sections.

SCOPE_INFLATION
  The concept's referential scope expands silently
  between appearances.
  Example: "community" shifts from "study community"
  to "communities generally" to "society."

AUTHORITY_TRANSFER
  The concept's epistemic authority is borrowed
  from its source domain and transferred to
  the manuscript's domain without bridge language.
  Example: "entropy" borrowed from physics
  applied to social systems as if the
  thermodynamic properties transfer.

REIFICATION
  An analytical concept is treated as a real entity
  rather than an analytical tool.
  Example: "discourse" shifts from "a way of reading"
  to "a force that shapes behavior."

EPISTEMIC_LOADING
  The concept accumulates argumentative weight
  beyond what its definition and evidence support —
  it becomes a shorthand for claims the manuscript
  cannot otherwise make.
```

---

## 5. FINAL EPISTEMIC ROLE ASSESSMENT

After tracking the full lifecycle, each concept's
final epistemic role is assessed:

```
GROUNDED_CARRIER
  Concept reaches epistemic carrier status
  with adequate definition and evidence support.
  Signal: LOW — expected behavior in strong theoretical work.

PARTIALLY_GROUNDED_CARRIER
  Concept reaches epistemic carrier status
  with partial definition and evidence support.
  Signal: MEDIUM.

UNGROUNDED_CARRIER
  Concept reaches epistemic carrier status
  without adequate definition or evidence support.
  It performs argumentative functions it was never equipped for.
  Signal: HIGH.

DECORATIVE_CONCEPT
  Concept introduced but never reaches carrier status.
  Present in framing and conclusion but absent from analysis.
  Signal: MEDIUM — ABSENT at STAGE 6.

STABLE_CONCEPT
  Concept introduced, defined, applied, and concluded
  without significant transformation or loading.
  Signal: none — expected behavior.
```

---

## 6. MANUSCRIPT-LEVEL PATTERNS

When multiple concepts show similar lifecycle trajectories,
a manuscript-level pattern is assembled:

```
CONCEPT_LOADING_PATTERN
  Multiple concepts reach UNGROUNDED_CARRIER status.
  Manuscript relies on concept accumulation
  rather than argument construction.
  Signal: HIGH — reported in SECTION 4.

CONCEPT_DECORATION_PATTERN
  Multiple concepts are DECORATIVE —
  introduced for framing but absent from analysis.
  Manuscript's theoretical apparatus is surface-level.
  Signal: MEDIUM — reported in SECTION 4.

SEMANTIC_DRIFT_PATTERN
  Multiple concepts show SEMANTIC_DRIFT events.
  Manuscript's conceptual vocabulary is unstable
  across its full length.
  Signal: MEDIUM-HIGH — reported in SECTION 4.
```

Pattern threshold: two or more concepts required.
Single-concept signals report in SECTION 2 only.

## 9. Q-VARIANT CALIBRATION

**Q1:**
All five concepts tracked at full lifecycle depth.
UNGROUNDED_CARRIER status explicitly surfaced.
CONCEPT_LOADING_PATTERN noted as potential desk-reject signal.
UNDEFINED definition at STAGE 1 flagged at HIGH sensitivity.
AUTHORITY_TRANSFER flagged at HIGH sensitivity.
UNMARKED_EXTENSION in STAGE 3 flagged.
Q1 reviewers weigh conceptual grounding closely in theoretical work.

**Q2:**
Full lifecycle tracking at standard depth.
UNGROUNDED_CARRIER reported.
SEMANTIC_DRIFT events reported.
DECORATIVE_CONCEPT reported without escalation.
UNMARKED_EXTENSION noted.

**Q3:**
UNGROUNDED_CARRIER status reported.
CONCEPT_LOADING_PATTERN reported.
Individual transformation events at MEDIUM signal suppressed.
STABLE_CONCEPT confirmed silently.
DECORATIVE_CONCEPT reported at summary level.

## 11. COLLISION MANAGEMENT

### With Concept-Evidence Bridge Kit (33):

Concept-Evidence Bridge Kit reads static grounding status:
is a concept GROUNDED / PARTIAL / FLOATING at any given point?

This kit reads dynamic lifecycle:
how does a concept's grounding status change across the manuscript?

Both active simultaneously.
Concept-Evidence Bridge governs point-in-time grounding signals;
this kit governs longitudinal trajectory signals.
Where both fire on the same concept:
→ Both reported as separate sub-signals in SECTION 2.
→ Not merged.

---

### With Epistemic Frame Kit (47):

Epistemic Frame Kit reads the operative epistemological frame.
This kit reads how individual concepts carry epistemic functions.

EPISTEMIC_LOADING transformation events cross-reference kit 47:
When a concept becomes an UNGROUNDED_CARRIER and the manuscript's
frame is INVISIBLE or produces FRAME_UNIVERSALIZATION,
both kits report in SECTION 2.
Not merged; collision not surfaced to user.

---

### With LENSSA-DEPTH Kit (11):

LENSSA-DEPTH reads numerical signal intensity, distribution,
and scale-shift patterns.
This kit reads conceptual lifecycle in the verbal layer.

In VERBAL-PRIMARY manuscripts these operate in parallel:
LENSSA-DEPTH on any numerical claims present;
this kit on conceptual behavior.
In QUANTITATIVE-PRIMARY manuscripts:
LENSSA-DEPTH is the primary reading layer;
this kit activates on framing zones only.
Not merged.

---

### With Argument Chain Kit (30):

Argument Chain Kit tracks the central claim longitudinally.
This kit tracks key concepts longitudinally.

Both active simultaneously; different objects of tracking.
Where CONCEPT_DECORATION_PATTERN co-occurs with
BROKEN or UNRESOLVED argument chain profile:
→ Both reported in their respective sections.
→ Not merged.

---

### With Verbal Lens Kit (10) Independent Mode:

In VERBAL-PRIMARY manuscripts, Verbal Lens Independent Mode
reads generalization scope signals at the language level.
This kit reads SCOPE_INFLATION as a concept-level event.

Both may detect scope expansion simultaneously:
→ Verbal Lens: GENERALIZATION_SCOPE signal in SECTION 6
→ This kit: SCOPE_INFLATION in SECTION 2
→ Different levels of analysis; both reported; not merged.

---

## 12. REVISION ROUND INTERACTION

In Revision Round Mode, concept lifecycle signals
carry additional weight under Q1-level sensitivity.

UNGROUNDED_CARRIER status in revised manuscripts
is read as residual risk — ungrounded epistemic carriers
are frequently identified in R1 reviewer comments
as "conceptual vagueness" or "theoretical underdevelopment."

SEMANTIC_DRIFT events in revised manuscripts
may indicate that revision introduced new meaning
to existing concepts without updating their definition —
a specific post-revision risk.

No separate flag produced.
Profile reported as normal; sensitivity applies implicitly.

---

## 13. HARD LIMITS

This kit must never:
  Evaluate whether concept evolution is theoretically appropriate
  Judge whether the concepts chosen are correct for the topic
  Recommend that specific concepts be redefined or replaced
  Label the manuscript as theoretically weak or sophisticated
  Produce a concept quality score
  Attribute intent to concept transformation events
  Act as a standalone theoretical review tool
  Generate independent sections outside the routing table

---

## 14. WHAT THIS KIT DOES NOT DO

Does not:
  Evaluate theoretical depth or sophistication
  Recommend conceptual revisions
  Compare concept use to field standards
  Assess whether the concepts chosen are appropriate
  Produce rankings of concept quality
  Replace Concept-Evidence Bridge Kit (33) —
    both kits serve different analytical functions

---

## 15. CANONICAL DEFINITION

CONCEPT LIFECYCLE KIT v1.0 tracks the full lifecycle of
up to five key concepts across a manuscript — mapping
introduction, definition, application, extension,
metaphorization, epistemic carrier status, and conclusion role —
detecting semantic drift, scope inflation, authority transfer,
reification, and epistemic loading as transformation events —
assembling a per-concept lifecycle profile and
manuscript-level concept behavior pattern (CONCEPT_LOADING,
CONCEPT_DECORATION, SEMANTIC_DRIFT) —
routing signals to SECTION 2, SECTION 4, and SECTION 6 —
without producing judgment, evaluation, or recommendation.
