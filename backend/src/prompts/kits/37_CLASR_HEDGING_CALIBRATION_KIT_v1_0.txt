## 1. PURPOSE (ONE SENTENCE)

To surface whether hedging is applied consistently
and proportionately across the manuscript's claims —
mapping selective hedging, proximity drift, compound
performative hedging, and calibration failures —
as structural consistency signals, without producing
evaluation of the manuscript's rigor.

---

## 2. DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Verbal Lens reads where language inflates specific claims.
FREQUENCY SIGNAL reads repetition density of hedge markers.
SECTION 6 reads overall hedging level: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW.

None of these reads hedging consistency — whether
the same type of claim receives the same hedging treatment
across the manuscript.

A manuscript may have appropriate hedging overall
yet apply it selectively: strong claims hedge-free,
weak claims over-hedged. Or it may hedge consistently
but at the wrong proximity — hedge appears three paragraphs
after the claim it nominally qualifies.

These patterns are not visible from hedging level alone.
They require a calibration read across claim types.

```
Claim types classified across manuscript
         ↓
Hedging presence and type mapped per claim
         ↓
Consistency across same-type claims assessed
         ↓
Proximity of hedge to claim measured
         ↓
Compound hedging structures assessed
         ↓
Calibration profile assembled
         ↓
Profile reported — no verdict
```

## 3. CLAIM TYPE CLASSIFICATION

For calibration, claims are classified into four types:

```
EMPIRICAL    → claims about what the data shows
               ("results indicate", "findings suggest")

CAUSAL       → claims about why or how
               ("X leads to", "because of", "driven by")

CONTRIBUTORY → claims about what the study adds
               ("this study demonstrates", "we show that",
               "to our knowledge this is the first")

IMPLICATIVE  → claims about what the findings mean
               beyond the study scope
               ("these findings suggest that", "this has implications for")
```

Each claim type has a calibration expectation:
- EMPIRICAL: hedging level proportionate to evidence strength
- CAUSAL: maximum hedging unless causal design present
- CONTRIBUTORY: hedging proportionate to novelty evidence
- IMPLICATIVE: hedging increases with scope of implication

## 4. HEDGING SIGNALS READ

### 4.1 Selective Hedging

The same claim type receives different hedging treatment
in different locations — some instances hedged, others not.

Signal indicators:
- CAUSAL claim hedged in methods: "may be related to"
  Same causal claim unhedged in conclusion: "demonstrates that X causes Y"
- CONTRIBUTORY claim hedged in introduction: "to our knowledge"
  Same contribution claim unhedged in abstract: "this is the first study"
- EMPIRICAL claims hedged in results but not in abstract

Label: SELECTIVE_HEDGING
Location: [zones where inconsistency detected]
Intensity: [LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH]

---

### 4.2 Proximity Drift

A hedge is present but positioned too far from the claim
it nominally qualifies to function as a genuine constraint.

Signal indicators:
- "These results should be interpreted with caution"
  appearing at the end of a section, after multiple
  un-hedged claims have been made in that section
- Limitation statement in SECTION 8 that nominally applies
  to a claim made in the abstract but is too distal
  to function as a genuine hedge
- "While this study has limitations" as a section opener
  followed by confident claims with no proximate hedging

Label: PROXIMITY_DRIFT
Location: [transition where drift occurs]
Intensity: [LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH]

---

### 4.3 Compound Performative Hedging

Multiple hedging devices are stacked on a single claim
in a way that performs caution without substantively
constraining the claim.

Signal indicators:
- "It might possibly be suggested that X could perhaps..."
  (four hedging devices on one claim — the accumulation
  paradoxically weakens each individual hedge)
- "While acknowledging the limitations of this study,
  and recognizing that further research is needed,
  it seems reasonable to conclude that X demonstrates Y"
  (performative caution framing around an unhedged conclusion)
- Hedging vocabulary without epistemic content:
  "interesting" / "notable" / "remarkable" used to
  introduce strong claims without reducing their certainty

Label: COMPOUND_PERFORMATIVE_HEDGING
Intensity: [LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH]

---

### 4.4 Calibration Failure

The hedging level applied to a claim is structurally
disproportionate to the evidence available — either
over-hedged (claim weaker than evidence supports)
or under-hedged (claim stronger than evidence supports).

Signal indicators — under-hedging:
- Strong causal language for observational findings
- Universal scope language for bounded sample
- "Confirms" where "is consistent with" is warranted
- "Demonstrates" where "suggests" is warranted

Signal indicators — over-hedging:
- Excessive caution for well-established findings
- "May possibly" for direct experimental results
- Hedging that exceeds what methodological limits require
  (self-suppression pattern)

Label: CALIBRATION_FAILURE
Direction: [UNDER_HEDGED / OVER_HEDGED]
Intensity: [LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH]

---

### 4.5 Hedge Stripping

A hedged claim in one zone appears without its hedge
in another zone — the hedge is "stripped" as the claim
moves through the manuscript.

Signal indicators:
- Results: "may suggest that X is associated with Y"
  Discussion: "X is associated with Y" (hedge removed)
  Conclusion: "X drives Y" (both hedge and verb softened)
- Abstract: "preliminary evidence indicates"
  Conclusion: "evidence confirms"

Label: HEDGE_STRIPPING
Path: [zone where hedge present → zone where hedge absent]
Intensity: [LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH]

---

## 5. HEDGING CALIBRATION PROFILE

After all signals are assessed, a calibration profile
is assembled:

```
CONSISTENT      → hedging applied evenly across same claim types;
                  no selective, proximity, or stripping signals
PARTIALLY_CONSISTENT → one hedging signal detected at LOW intensity
SELECTIVE       → same claim type receives inconsistent hedging
                  across manuscript zones
PERFORMATIVE    → hedging is structurally present but
                  rhetorically hollow across multiple instances
DRIFTING        → hedge stripping or proximity drift
                  detected across multiple transitions
UNCALIBRATED    → calibration failures present across
                  multiple claim types
```

## 7. Q-VARIANT CALIBRATION

Q1:
  All five signal types active at full sensitivity.
  SELECTIVE_HEDGING and HEDGE_STRIPPING explicitly surfaced.
  UNCALIBRATED profile noted as potential reviewer concern.
  UNDER_HEDGED calibration failure flagged at highest sensitivity —
  Q1 reviewers most sensitive to unhedged causal and
  contributory claims.

Q2:
  All signals active at standard sensitivity.
  SELECTIVE_HEDGING at MEDIUM and HIGH intensity reported.
  CALIBRATION_FAILURE at MODERATE reported.

Q3:
  HEDGE_STRIPPING and UNCALIBRATED profile reported.
  SELECTIVE_HEDGING at HIGH intensity reported.
  LOW intensity signals suppressed silently.
  OVER_HEDGED calibration failure suppressed.

## 8. PARTIAL INPUT BEHAVIOR

Abstract only (Type A):
→ CALIBRATION_FAILURE partially readable for abstract claims
→ HEDGE_STRIPPING not assessable (body absent)
→ Label: [Hedging scan — abstract only, reduced coverage]

Introduction only (Type B):
→ CONTRIBUTORY claim hedging readable
→ Cross-zone signals not assessable

Methods only (Type C):
→ EMPIRICAL claim hedging in methods readable
→ SELECTIVE_HEDGING partially assessable if
  methods and results both present

Results / Discussion only (Type D):
→ HEDGE_STRIPPING between results and discussion readable
→ PROXIMITY_DRIFT in discussion readable

Mixed (Type E):
→ Kit runs on available zones
→ Coverage noted per signal type

## 9. REVISION ROUND INTERACTION

In Revision Round Mode, hedging calibration signals
carry additional weight under Q1-level sensitivity.

HEDGE_STRIPPING and SELECTIVE_HEDGING in revised manuscripts
are read as residual risk — inconsistent hedging is
frequently identified in R1 reviewer comments as
"overstating findings" or "inappropriate certainty."

COMPOUND_PERFORMATIVE_HEDGING in revised manuscripts
may indicate that hedging was added in response to
reviewers without substantive engagement with the
underlying claim.

No separate flag produced.
Signal reported as normal; sensitivity applies implicitly.

## 11. WHAT THIS KIT DOES NOT DO

Does not:
  Evaluate whether the hedging level is appropriate
  Judge whether the manuscript is sufficiently cautious
  Recommend specific hedging language
  Label any claim as over- or under-stated
  Rewrite or suggest rewriting of hedged claims
  Generate independent flags outside SECTION 6, SECTION 4, SECTION 8

---

## 12. HARD LIMITS

This kit must never:
  Produce hedging adequacy verdicts
  Recommend specific hedge insertion or removal
  Label a manuscript as overconfident or under-confident
  Override Verbal Lens or FREQUENCY SIGNAL outputs
  Generate independent sections

---

## 13. CANONICAL DEFINITION

HEDGING-CALIBRATION KIT v1.0 surfaces whether hedging
is applied consistently and proportionately across
the manuscript's claim types — mapping selective hedging,
proximity drift, compound performative hedging,
calibration failures, and hedge stripping — assembling
a calibration profile of CONSISTENT, PARTIALLY_CONSISTENT,
SELECTIVE, PERFORMATIVE, DRIFTING, or UNCALIBRATED —
without producing evaluation, verdict, or recommendation.
