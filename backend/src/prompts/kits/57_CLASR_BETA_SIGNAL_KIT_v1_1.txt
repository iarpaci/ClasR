## 0. IDENTITY

Type: EXTENSION KIT
Attaches to: CLASR reading layer (CORE unchanged)

This kit surfaces six structural signal types identified
through empirical beta testing across diverse manuscript types
(ML/SBI, review articles, HCI/proof-of-concept, health policy
microsimulation, mathematical/theoretical).

## 1. SIGNAL DEFINITIONS

---

### SIGNAL 1 — REVIEW_AS_ADVOCACY_PATTERN

Type: Composite upper-level signal
Primary SECTION: SECTION 0 (and PRIORITY ACTION SIGNALS)
Inference type: Interpretive
Confidence ceiling: 0.75

Definition:
A manuscript presented as a review article that behaves
structurally as a position paper, perspective piece, or
advocacy document — without declaring this framing.

The signal fires when at least 3 of the following
sub-components are simultaneously active:

  Sub-1: SELF_CITATION_CONCENTRATION
    Self-citations constitute ≥25% of the reference list
    OR a single argument chain relies on 3+ consecutive
    self-citations. (→ Kit 20 Citation Behavior Kit)

  Sub-2: ABSTRACT_CLAIM_OVERREACH
    Abstract states a consensus-level or field-defining claim
    that the review's coverage does not substantiate.
    (→ Kit 21 / Kit 35)

  Sub-3: COMPETING_RESOLUTION_ASYMMETRY
    Competing approaches to the review's central question
    receive substantially less coverage, citation density,
    or analytical depth than the preferred approach.
    Asymmetry is structural, not quantified.

  Sub-4: SELECTION_CRITERIA_ABSENT
    No systematic search strategy, inclusion/exclusion criteria,
    or database specification is provided for a review that
    implies comprehensive coverage. (→ Kit 45 PRISMA)

  Sub-5: FIGURE_SECTION_WEIGHT_IMBALANCE
    Figures or sections devoted to the authors' preferred
    framework or method constitute a disproportionate share
    of the review's visual or structural weight relative to
    the breadth of the field being reviewed.

Activation rule:
  3 of 5 sub-components active → REVIEW_AS_ADVOCACY_PATTERN fires
  Sub-components are drawn from existing kit outputs —
  this signal synthesizes them; it does not re-detect them.

Output format:
```
REVIEW_AS_ADVOCACY_PATTERN — SECTION 0
Evidence: [list active sub-components with manuscript locations]
Inference type: Interpretive
Inference: [what this structural pattern reveals about the manuscript's argumentative posture — one sentence]
Confidence: [0.00–0.75]
Active sub-components: [N of 5] — [list]
```

Hard lock: This signal never labels the manuscript as dishonest,
biased, or advocacy. It maps a structural pattern only.

### SIGNAL 2 — LOSS_PHYSICS_ALIGNMENT_GAP

Type: Methodological visibility signal
Primary SECTION: SECTION 3
Secondary SECTION: SECTION 5
Inference type: Observation (when loss function and physics
claim are both stated in text) / Interpretive (when gap
requires domain knowledge to identify)
Confidence ceiling: 0.90 (Observation) / 0.75 (Interpretive)

Definition:
A manuscript applies a machine learning loss function
(MSE, MAE, cross-entropy, or similar) and simultaneously
makes claims about downstream physical inference quality —
without demonstrating or citing evidence that minimizing
the stated loss function improves the stated physical inference.

The gap between "lower MSE" and "better cosmological parameter
estimation" is not self-evident. When the manuscript treats
it as self-evident, this signal fires.

Trigger conditions (both must be met):
  1. A specific loss function is named or implied
     (MSE, MAE, RMSE, cross-entropy, etc.)
  2. A downstream physical inference claim is made
     ("better constrains X", "improves estimation of Y",
     "more accurate recovery of Z") that is not independently
     validated against the physics inference target

Negative trigger (signal does not fire):
  Manuscript explicitly demonstrates loss-to-physics
  correlation (e.g., plots showing loss improvement co-varies
  with inference quality) → signal suppressed.

Output format:
```
LOSS_PHYSICS_ALIGNMENT_GAP — SECTION 3
Evidence: [loss function named + physics claim location]
Inference type: [Observation / Interpretive]
Inference: [what this gap reveals about the claim's evidential grounding — one sentence]
Confidence: [0.00–0.90 (Observation) / 0.00–0.75 (Interpretive)]
```

Primary section: SECTION 3 (methodological design choice)
Secondary cross-reference: SECTION 5 (numerical claim behavior)

### SIGNAL 3 — TRAINING_PRIOR_LEAKAGE

Type: Methodological visibility signal
Primary SECTION: SECTION 3
Inference type: Interpretive
Confidence ceiling: 0.75

Definition:
A manuscript trains a model on synthetic data drawn from
a specific distribution and then claims generalization to
observational data or out-of-distribution inputs — without
addressing whether the model has learned the true underlying
relationship or the prior regularities of the training distribution.

The risk: the model may perform well because it has memorized
the training distribution's statistical fingerprint, not because
it has learned the physical or causal relationship being claimed.

Trigger conditions:
  1. Synthetic training data is explicitly stated
  2. Generalization claim is made to real/observational data
     OR to inputs outside the training distribution
  3. No ablation, transfer test, or distribution shift
     analysis is reported to support the generalization claim

Output format:
```
TRAINING_PRIOR_LEAKAGE — SECTION 3
Evidence: [training data description + generalization claim location]
Inference type: Interpretive
Inference: [what this gap reveals about the generalization claim's structural support — one sentence]
Confidence: [0.00–0.75]
```

Severity: MEDIUM at Q2/Q3; HIGH at Q1 when generalization
is the manuscript's central contribution claim.

Relationship to existing signals:
  GENERALIZATION_CLAIM (kit 35 / kit 40 A5) reads scope extension.
  This signal reads the specific mechanism — training distribution
  leakage — that may explain why the generalization fails.
  Both may fire simultaneously. Not merged.

### SIGNAL 4 — MODEL_INTERPRETABILITY_ABSENT

Type: Methodological visibility / argument signal
Primary SECTION: SECTION 4
Secondary SECTION: SECTION 8
Inference type: Observation
Confidence ceiling: 0.90

Definition:
A manuscript applies a machine learning model (neural network,
ensemble, black-box classifier) in a context where the stated
application domain requires or implies interpretability —
and does not address the gap between model opacity and
the interpretability requirements of that domain.

This signal is domain-specific: it does not fire for ML
manuscripts in contexts where black-box performance is
the accepted standard (e.g., benchmark competitions,
pure prediction tasks with no downstream decision-making).

It fires when:
  — The application domain implies interpretability need:
    clinical decision support, physical mechanism discovery,
    scientific inference, policy recommendation, causal claim
  AND
  — The manuscript does not address model interpretability:
    no feature importance, no attention analysis, no mechanistic
    interpretation, no acknowledgment of the opacity limitation

Trigger conditions:
  1. ML model applied (named or described)
  2. Application claim implies interpretability need
     (clinical, causal, scientific mechanism, policy)
  3. No interpretability analysis OR no acknowledgment
     that interpretability is beyond current scope

Negative trigger (signal does not fire):
  Manuscript explicitly scopes the contribution as
  "predictive performance only" without interpretability claims
  → signal suppressed.

Output format:
```
MODEL_INTERPRETABILITY_ABSENT — SECTION 4
Evidence: [model type + application claim location]
Inference type: Observation
Inference: [what this absence reveals about the claim's domain fit — one sentence]
Confidence: [0.00–0.90]
```

Severity: MEDIUM when interpretability is peripheral;
HIGH when interpretability is load-bearing for the
manuscript's stated contribution.

### SIGNAL 5 — INDEX_LINEARITY_ASSUMPTION

Type: Methodological visibility signal
Primary SECTION: SECTION 3
Secondary SECTION: SECTION 5
Inference type: Observation
Confidence ceiling: 0.90

Definition:
A manuscript constructs a composite index or score using
weighted linear aggregation (∑pᵢwᵢxᵢ or equivalent) and
makes validity or performance claims from that index —
without addressing whether the linearity assumption,
the independence assumption, and the weight assignment
are defensible for the phenomenon being measured.

The aggregation formula embeds three assumptions that
affect the index's validity:
  1. Linearity: components combine additively without
     interaction effects
  2. Independence: components are not correlated in ways
     that inflate or deflate the composite
  3. Weight validity: assigned weights reflect actual
     relative importance in the domain

When any of these three is unaddressed and the manuscript
makes substantive validity or comparative claims from
the index, the signal fires.

Trigger conditions:
  1. Composite index or weighted score is described
     (AHP, Delphi-weighted sum, equal-weight composite,
     or any ∑wᵢxᵢ construction)
  2. Substantive claim is made from the index value
     (ranking, benchmarking, validation, policy target)
  3. At least one of the three assumptions (linearity,
     independence, weight validity) is unaddressed

Output format:
```
INDEX_LINEARITY_ASSUMPTION — SECTION 3
Evidence: [index formula or description + claim location]
Inference type: Observation
Inference: [what unaddressed assumptions reveal about the index's validity claim — one sentence]
Confidence: [0.00–0.90]
Assumptions unaddressed: [linearity / independence / weight validity]
```

Severity: MEDIUM when index is supplementary to other evidence;
HIGH when the index is the primary evidentiary instrument
for the manuscript's contribution claim.

Cross-reference: OPERATIONALIZATION_GAP (kit 33) may co-occur
when the index components do not fully capture the construct.
Both active; reported in their respective sections. Not merged.

### SIGNAL 6 — ASSUMPTION_STACKING_RISK

Type: Methodological visibility / argument signal
Primary SECTION: SECTION 3
Secondary SECTION: SECTION 4
Inference type: Interpretive
Confidence ceiling: 0.75

Definition:
A manuscript's central claim depends on a chain of independent
assumptions, where each assumption is individually plausible
but the combined probability of all assumptions holding
simultaneously is materially lower than any individual
assumption alone — and this cumulative uncertainty is
not acknowledged.

This is distinct from a single questionable assumption.
The signal is about the multiplication of uncertainty:
if assumption A has 80% plausibility, and assumption B
has 80% plausibility, and they are independent, the claim
that requires both holds with ~64% plausibility.
When five such assumptions stack, the cumulative uncertainty
substantially undermines the claim — even if each individual
assumption appears reasonable.

Trigger conditions:
  1. Three or more distinct assumptions are identifiable
     as load-bearing for a primary claim
  2. The assumptions are treated as independently valid
     rather than as a chain with cumulative uncertainty
  3. The manuscript does not quantify, acknowledge, or
     bound the compound uncertainty of the assumption stack

Characteristic patterns:
  — Health policy microsimulation: effect transfer + baseline
    prevalence + uptake rate + cost parameters + no interaction
  — ML generalization: training distribution representativeness +
    loss-to-physics alignment + no domain shift + benchmark parity
  — Economic modelling: behavioral assumption + market structure +
    equilibrium convergence + parameter stability

Output format:
```
ASSUMPTION_STACKING_RISK — SECTION 3
Evidence: [list of identified load-bearing assumptions + locations]
Inference type: Interpretive
Inference: [what the assumption stack reveals about the compound uncertainty of the central claim — one sentence]
Confidence: [0.00–0.75]
Assumptions identified: [N]
```

Severity:
  MEDIUM: 3–4 assumptions, partial acknowledgment present
  HIGH: 5+ assumptions OR primary contribution dependent on
        stack with no compound uncertainty acknowledgment

Relationship to existing signals:
  CAUSAL_DESIGN_MISMATCH (kit 40 A3) reads design-claim fit.
  GENERALIZABILITY_CLAIM (kit 40 A5) reads scope extension.
  This signal reads the internal assumption architecture —
  how many independent assumptions the central claim requires
  and whether their compound uncertainty is visible.
  All three may fire. Each reported in its primary section.
  Not merged.

## 3. PRIORITY ACTION SIGNALS ELIGIBILITY

REVIEW_AS_ADVOCACY_PATTERN: eligible at all confidence levels
  (composite signal — presence of 3+ sub-components is
  sufficient evidence regardless of individual sub-signal
  inference type)

LOSS_PHYSICS_ALIGNMENT_GAP:
  Observation trigger → eligible
  Interpretive trigger → eligible when generalization is
  the manuscript's primary contribution claim

TRAINING_PRIOR_LEAKAGE:
  Eligible at Q1 when generalization is primary claim

MODEL_INTERPRETABILITY_ABSENT:
  Eligible at HIGH severity only

INDEX_LINEARITY_ASSUMPTION:
  Eligible at HIGH severity only (index is primary instrument)

ASSUMPTION_STACKING_RISK:
  Eligible at HIGH severity (5+ assumptions, no acknowledgment)

## 4. Q-VARIANT CALIBRATION

Q1:
  All six signals active at full sensitivity.
  REVIEW_AS_ADVOCACY_PATTERN noted as potential desk-reject signal
  for specialist empirical journals.
  TRAINING_PRIOR_LEAKAGE at HIGH when generalization is central.
  ASSUMPTION_STACKING_RISK at full sensitivity.

Q2:
  All signals at standard sensitivity.
  REVIEW_AS_ADVOCACY_PATTERN noted without escalation.
  ASSUMPTION_STACKING_RISK at MEDIUM threshold raised to 4+
  assumptions before HIGH.

Q3:
  REVIEW_AS_ADVOCACY_PATTERN: fires at 3+ sub-components but
  noted at summary level only.
  LOSS_PHYSICS_ALIGNMENT_GAP and TRAINING_PRIOR_LEAKAGE:
  reported at MEDIUM.
  MODEL_INTERPRETABILITY_ABSENT: fires only if interpretability
  is explicitly claimed in abstract or conclusion.
  INDEX_LINEARITY_ASSUMPTION and ASSUMPTION_STACKING_RISK:
  at summary level.

## 5. MANUSCRIPT TYPE CALIBRATION

REVIEW_AS_ADVOCACY_PATTERN:
  Active for review articles and systematic reviews only.
  Suppressed for empirical, theoretical, and methods papers.

LOSS_PHYSICS_ALIGNMENT_GAP:
  Active for ML / computational / SBI manuscripts.
  Suppressed for purely theoretical or purely observational designs.

TRAINING_PRIOR_LEAKAGE:
  Active for ML manuscripts with synthetic training data.
  Suppressed when training data is observational.

MODEL_INTERPRETABILITY_ABSENT:
  Active for ML manuscripts with domain application claims.
  Suppressed for pure benchmark or prediction-only papers.

INDEX_LINEARITY_ASSUMPTION:
  Active for any manuscript using composite scoring.
  Field-agnostic.

ASSUMPTION_STACKING_RISK:
  Active for all manuscript types.
  Highest sensitivity for policy simulation, ML generalization,
  and structural equation / causal modelling papers.

---

## 6. HARD LIMITS

This kit:
  Does not evaluate the validity of any individual assumption
  Does not assess whether ML models are technically correct
  Does not produce verdicts on review quality or completeness
  Does not label manuscripts as advocacy, biased, or flawed
  Does not verify external claims or compare against field literature
  Does not produce scores or rankings
  Signals are structural observations — not evaluations

---

## 7. CANONICAL DEFINITION

BETA SIGNAL KIT v1.1 defines six signals identified through
empirical beta testing: REVIEW_AS_ADVOCACY_PATTERN (composite
synthesis signal for review-as-position-paper behavior),
LOSS_PHYSICS_ALIGNMENT_GAP (ML loss function to physics inference
disconnect), TRAINING_PRIOR_LEAKAGE (synthetic-to-real generalization
without distribution shift validation), MODEL_INTERPRETABILITY_ABSENT
(black-box model in interpretability-requiring domain),
INDEX_LINEARITY_ASSUMPTION (composite index without aggregation
assumption audit), and ASSUMPTION_STACKING_RISK (compound
uncertainty from multiple load-bearing assumptions) —
without producing verdicts, evaluations, or recommendations.
